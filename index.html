Methodology

Next Dollar Impact.

Most accessibility reports tell you what is wrong. Next Dollar Impact tells you what to fix first, ranked by how many compliance points each remediation delivers per dollar spent.

This matters when budgets are constrained. The first $25,000 you spend should close the most certification gap, not the most work and not the most convenient items.

“The purpose of the report is not documentation. It is a decision-support tool. Every section should make the next capital decision easier.”

The mandatory life-safety work recovers 15 of the 47 points needed; these six actions, totalling $3,760–$6,370, recover 35 more and pass the RHF Accessibility Certified threshold. FAQ

Common questions, direct answers.

What areas do you serve?

Novo Accessibility serves organizations across all of Canada.

What does a pilot cost?

A three-to-five facility pilot is $8,000 plus GST. One fixed fee, quoted in writing before work starts, covering every facility in that range — three, four or five costs the same. It assumes ordinary civic buildings up to about 20,000 square feet and three storeys, with no pool, arena or residential units; anything larger or more complex is quoted separately. Travel is included within 100 km of our office; beyond that, travel time and expenses are quoted with the engagement and billed at cost against receipts.

Can this report be used for a grant application?

Yes. The scored findings, phased capital plan, and certification pathway table are built with grant writers in mind. See the Funding section for the two programmes that apply and their current status.

Does the sample report show everything we would receive?

It shows three of the five deliverables in full — the prioritization of findings, the phased capital roadmap and the executive summary — for one illustrative library, with the roll-up across all five facilities. A pilot gives you that full treatment for every facility, plus the combined portfolio plan. The council briefing deck and the procurement package are produced from the same assessment data and are provided with an engagement rather than published as a sample.

Why is the sample report about a town that does not exist?

Because a client's assessment is theirs. Publishing a real municipality's barriers, scores and costs would be a breach of confidence no matter how useful it might be as marketing. Clearwater is fictional; the methodology, the clause references and the arithmetic behind it are exactly what a real engagement produces.

How long until we have the report?

A pilot runs six to eight weeks end to end. The written report is delivered within seven to ten business days of the final site visit.

How are findings measured and located?

Every measurable finding is taken on site with a Bosch GLM165-27CGL laser measure, accurate to 1/16 inch, and a force gauge and lux meter where the item calls for them. Each finding is then pinned to a floor plan and shaded by severity, so a reader who has never walked the building can see where the problems are. Floor plans drawn to measured dimensions are available as an extra, quoted separately before work begins.

What happens after the pilot?

Most municipalities have more than five facilities, so the usual next step is the rest of the portfolio on the same method. After the first year of work, a reassessment measures progress against the baseline. If you decide to pursue RHF certification, the report shows which prerequisites must be cleared first; certification itself is adjudicated by the Rick Hansen Foundation.
